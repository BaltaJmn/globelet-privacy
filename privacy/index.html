Globelet and your privacy

In force since 16 November 2026. Globelet is published by Baltasar Jimenez Rincon, a developer working alone in Spain.

The photos and videos you turn into planets are processed on your iPhone and are never sent to anyone, me included.

Your photos and videos

You choose each photo or video in Apple's own picker, which hands Globelet only the item you picked. Globelet cannot browse your library. The picture is projected by the iPhone's graphics chip and the result is written to a temporary file inside the app, which is deleted when you open another picture.

When you tap Save to Photos, iOS asks whether Globelet may add to your library. That permission is add-only: it does not let the app read or change what is already there. If you say no, you can still share the result from the share sheet.

Location, dates and other details stored inside your original files are not copied into what Globelet exports.

What the app remembers

Four small things, kept on the device and removed when you delete the app: how many planets you have saved, which version last asked you for a rating (so it asks at most once per version), whether the Pro screen has already been shown to you, and whether Pro is unlocked.

Buying Globelet Pro

Payments are Apple's business: the App Store charges your Apple Account and Globelet never learns your name, email or card. So that I can count sales, the app includes the RevenueCat SDK, which is told by the App Store that a purchase happened: which product, when, in which country, under a random identifier created for this app. It learns nothing about your pictures or how you use the app. Their policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

Things Globelet does not have

Accounts, ads, usage analytics, trackers, or any server of its own.

Young users

Globelet is a general photo tool and is not aimed at children under 13. As it collects no personal data, there is none to collect from them either.
